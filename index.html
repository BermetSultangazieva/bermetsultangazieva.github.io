  1. Jailbreaking LLMs for Copyrighted Content

    AI Safety & Ethics · LLM Security · MPhil Dissertation · 2025–2026

    My MPhil dissertation at the University of Cambridge, supervised by Professor Bill Byrne, in AI safety and ethics: investigating how adversarial prompting can drive large language models to reproduce copyrighted material — and how to defend against it.

    • Jailbreak robustness — how adversarial prompts bypass safety training, and how to defend against them.
    • Copyright and memorization — when and why models extract or reproduce protected training data.
    • Built on the HarmBench red-teaming framework to evaluate safety and alignment beyond benchmarks, toward real deployment risk.

  3. P/NP-Complete Classification of the Tait–Rolfsen Knot Table

    Theoretical CS · Knot Theory · Bard Summer Research Institute · Summer 2024

    One of four research assistants on Professor Robert W. McGrail's long-running ASCLab program (begun in 2008) at the Bard Summer Research Institute, working to classify the computational complexity of every knot in the 250-knot Tait–Rolfsen table.

    • The program associates to every finite algebra a class of constraint-satisfaction problems (CSPs): "nice" structures — groups, vector spaces, Boolean algebras — yield polynomial-time CSPs, while many others are NP-complete, motivating a conjectured P/NP-complete dichotomy.
    • Since 2008 the lab has linked three-dimensional knots to CSPs through quandle colorings, conjecturing — and, for quandles, proving — a P/NP-complete dichotomy for knots.
    • A special family of "Sharac" quandles forces NP-completeness: whenever a knot admits a non-trivial coloring by one of them, that knot must be NP-complete. Specialized software tests a knot's system of crossing equations against each quandle for such a coloring.
    • Designed and built a centralized research database from scratch to track progress, colorings, and annotated results across the multi-year project.
    • To date the team has verified 118 of the 249 non-trivial knots as NP-complete (97 fully annotated); the long-term goal is a 300–400-page volume devoting a page to each knot.
    • Co-authored the resulting manuscript, The Search for Sharac Quandles — accepted for presentation at SYNASC 2026.
